## 0. How the Operator uses this kit
1. Send §1 (Arabic) and §2 (English) together, with the intake form (`PARTNER_INTAKE_FORM.md`). The partner picks
   the language. Both say the same thing; if they differ, the version that is more protective for the partner applies.
2. The partner ticks each permission **separately**. Nothing is pre-ticked. Permission 3 is **OFF by default** and,
   even if ticked, is **unused** until an ADR lifts ADR-0020 and the partner is asked again.
3. A "signature" is: the partner's typed name + date + ticks, sent back through the form or as a WhatsApp/DM
   message quoting the kit version (`CK-v0.1`). Keep that record **outside git**, in the Founder's access-controlled
   storage. The repo may hold only: partner ID (`P-001`…), kit version, the three tick values, dates. No names, phones,
   handles or emails in git (DATA_POLICY item 2).
4. Each partner gets a **deletion code** (e.g. `P-003-K7`) in the confirmation message. It works with or without email.
5. Partner #1 is the Founder's own store. It still signs, so the record is uniform.

**Founding Creator Program: short-video editing pilot** · version CK-v0.1

**Who we are and what we ask:** We are a small team building a tool that edits short videos in Arabic. We ask for

**What we always do with your clips (the service itself):** we extract the spoken text, the scene cuts and a few
still frames, edit two or more versions for you and send them to you. This needs none of the permissions below.

**Who else sees your data (processors):**
- Our form tool: **<tool name filled in before sending>**, which stores your answers.
- GitHub / Codespaces (Microsoft): the cloud computer our tools run on, where your clips are processed temporarily.
  We never put your clips in a public repository.
- Anthropic (the Claude assistant): it may read **the spoken text and a few still frames** from your clips, never the
  full video, to suggest edits.

We do not sell your data or give it to anyone other than these.

**Permissions: choose each one separately. You can withdraw any of them later without affecting the others.**
- ☐ **(1) Evaluation:** we measure the quality of our edits on your clips (for example: are the captions right? is

  recipes. Nothing is published, and nothing is used to train AI models.
- ☐ **(3) Future model training (OFF by default):** we **do not train any model now**. Ticking this box is only an

**Your promises:**
- ☐ I confirm the clips are mine, or I have permission to use them this way.
- ☐ I confirm that **everyone seen or heard** in the clips agreed to appear, and I can show this if asked.

**Retention and deletion:** we keep your clips and everything derived from them for **at most 90 days from the date we

## 4. Withdrawal and deletion procedure (Operator side)
1. On a keyword: match the code to the partner ID; reply with a receipt within 24 h.
2. "WITHDRAW n": set that permission to false from that date; stop the matching use. Results already aggregated
   (anonymous totals) stay. "WITHDRAW 1" also removes the partner's rows from any eval table not yet aggregated.
3. "DELETE": delete source clips, transcripts, keyframes, annotations, edits, feedback, and exports from the form
   tool, Codespace (`quarantine/`, `cache/<video_id>/`, `out/`), and any local copy. Check git: nothing of theirs is
   committed (it should never be). Log `date · partner ID · what was deleted · by whom` in the consent register (outside git)
   and one line without personal data in DATA_RIGHTS (row status → DELETED).
4. Day-90 sweep: on day 80 the Operator asks the partner "renew or delete?". No answer by day 90 = delete.
5. Every annotation record carries `rights_id` and the three permission flags; a pipeline stage must refuse a record
   whose needed flag is false (Engineering implements this from the field mapping in H-0034-return).

## 5. Lawyer before scale, and why not before day 1
Not needed before pilot day 1, **if**: ≤ 5 known partners (Founder network), no payment, permission 3 unused,
no clip with minors, nothing published by us, and records kept outside git. These keep the risk small and reversible.
A lawyer must review these **[LAWYER]** points before more partners, any paid use, or any public launch:
1. Law 81/2018: whether our processing needs a prior declaration or licence with the Lebanese authorities, and
   the lawful basis. Not verified from the primary legal text in this brief.
2. Cross-border transfer: processors are outside Lebanon (US); partners may be in KSA/UAE (PDPL) or the EU (GDPR).
3. Whether a typed name + WhatsApp message is valid consent evidence.
4. The partner warranty for people in clips: is it enough, or do we need signed releases from each person?
5. Permission 3 (training): the wording must be re-drafted and re-consented before any use.
6. Anthropic's data terms for the Founder's Claude plan: the Founder must confirm, in the account's privacy settings,
   that conversations are **not** used for model training. **Unverified by Assurance**; until confirmed, do not show
   partner transcripts or keyframes to Claude.
